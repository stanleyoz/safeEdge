# Staged near-miss session — plan

**Purpose:** test one hypothesis with enough episodes to settle it. On the July
clip, V-JEPA 2.1 (ViT-g predictor) anticipated WARNING 2 s ahead with AUROC
0.70 [0.55, 0.84] over 8 episodes, and the highest-scoring pre-warning windows
showed pedestrians the detector could not yet see: entering at the crop edge,
or about to step out from between parked cars. If that is the mechanism, the
world model should anticipate **occluded** and **edge** entries better than
**visible** approaches, and better than pixel motion.

**Shape of the day:** ~2 h on site, 4 people, 80 scripted trials plus 20 min of
calm footage. The moving car never comes near a person. "Near-miss" here is a
geometric event in the camera's view, never a real one.

---

## 1. Why the design looks like this

Three facts from the July analysis drive it:

1. **WARNING fires when a pedestrian enters the AOI strip.** The strip is lined
   with parked cars, so any pedestrian in it is within 4 m of one. No calm
   window in July contained a detected pedestrian. The *event* is therefore
   "pedestrian enters the strip"; no moving car is needed to produce it, which
   is what makes the session safe.
2. **Anticipation needs calm time before each event.** A window spans 3.75 s
   and the horizon is 2–4 s, so each entry must follow **≥ 15 s with nobody in
   the strip**. This sets the trial pace.
3. **Eight episodes give a CI of ±0.15.** Twenty per condition should roughly
   halve that, enough to compare occluded/edge against visible.

## 2. Conditions

| Code | Condition | What the actor does | Hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **O1** | Occluded step-out | Waits hidden between two parked cars on the far side, then steps into the lane | Surprise rises before detection |
| **O2** | Edge entry | Starts outside the frame, walks in at the left/right edge and straight into the lane | Surprise rises before detection |
| **V1** | Visible approach (control) | Stands clearly in view at the far side for ≥ 5 s, then walks into the lane | Weaker anticipation: nothing hidden to anticipate |
| **V2** | Vehicle-led | Actor already standing in the lane ≥ 5 m from a parked car that then pulls out slowly | Intent cue: parked → moving |
| **N** | Negative control | Walks the foreground path only, never enters the lane | No WARNING; measures false alarms from near-field motion |

**80 trials** in 4 blocks of 20 (O1, O2, V1 × 5; V2 × 3; N × 2 per block),
shuffled within blocks, entry side and actor alternated:
[`run_sheet.csv`](run_sheet.csv), generated by
`make_run_sheet.py` (seed 20261120). Run them **in sheet order**; a skipped
trial is marked invalid, not re-ordered.

## 3. Safety (non-negotiable)

- **Site permission** from the car-park manager, in writing, with the date and
  time window. Choose a low-traffic period.
- **Roles:** a safety marshal whose only job is safety, with authority to stop
  any trial. Stop word: **"STOP"**; everyone freezes.
- **The car never moves toward a person.** It moves only in V2, at walking pace
  (≤ 5 km/h), pulling out *away from* the actor, with ≥ 5 m separation marked
  by cones. In O1, O2, V1 and N the car stays parked with the engine off.
- **No running**, no stepping out in front of anything moving, hi-vis vests on
  actors and marshal.
- **Members of the public:** pause the session if anyone (or any vehicle) not
  part of it enters the scene; mark the affected trial invalid and re-run it at
  the end of its block.

## 4. Privacy and consent

- Actors sign a short consent form covering recording, research use, and
  whether their likeness may appear in a publication (yes/no; default no).
- Bystanders: pause as above; their footage is not used and is not shared.
- Raw video stays off the public GitHub repo (as with all clips so far).
  Only metrics, traces and plots without people are committed.

## 5. People and kit

| Role | Who | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operator | You | Laptop, run sheet, calls each trial, shows the slate, watches the live view |
| Actor A, Actor B | 2 helpers | Alternate trials (two appearances reduce identity effects) |
| Driver | 1 helper (V2 only) | Moves the car on the operator's call |
| Safety marshal | 1 helper | Watches the scene, not the screen; owns "STOP" |

Kit: cones and chalk for marks; A4 **slate cards** (trial number, large, black on
white); hi-vis vests; printed run sheet; a phone stopwatch synced to the laptop
clock; two-way radios or phones for driver and marshal.

## 6. Recording setup (day before)

The Jetson's live pipeline keeps only a **rolling 5 × 60 s buffer**, so the
session must be recorded separately or it will be overwritten.

1. **Stop the live pipeline** on the Jetson for the session (it has run since
   14 Aug; note the exact command used to start it so it can be restored).
2. **Record full-frame raw video continuously** for the whole session, not the
   AOI crop: the crop is applied at analysis, so a wider configuration can be
   tested later without re-staging. Use
   `tools/capture_video.py --source webcam --no-preview` over SSH (or the edge
   loop with recording on and slot rotation off).
3. **Per-frame wall-clock timestamps:** `capture_video.py` writes
   `<out>.frames.csv` (frame, Unix wall-clock seconds, monotonic seconds) next
   to the video and prints the measured fps at the end. The July clips said
   15 fps in the header but ran at 7.7; the sidecar removes that guesswork.
   *Still to do: a short test capture on the Jetson.*
4. **Check the camera has not moved** since July: compare a fresh frame with the
   July frame and re-check two homography points with a tape measure (bay
   width ≈ 2.5 m). If it moved, recalibrate homography and AOI first.
5. **Disk:** ~2 h of 1280 × 720 at ~10 fps is a few GB; confirm space.
6. **Clock:** confirm the Jetson's NTP time; the run sheet's wall-clock column
   and the slate are the two sync paths.

## 7. On the day

**Arrive 45 min early.** Marks, cones, briefing, consent forms, a full dry run of
one trial per condition (not recorded as data).

**Calm footage (start, 10 min):** nobody staged in view. This calibrates τ and
fits the ViT-B ridge forecaster on the day's own lighting.

**Trial loop** (≈ 60 s each, 80 trials ≈ 80 min plus resets):

1. Operator checks the strip has been empty **≥ 15 s**; calls
   "Trial *n*, *condition*, *side*".
2. Holds the **slate** in view at the fixed spot on the grass (outside the AOI)
   for 2 s; notes the wall-clock time on the sheet.
3. Actor performs the condition; stays in the lane ~5 s; walks out the same way.
4. Operator marks the trial valid/invalid with a one-word note.

**Short break between blocks** (5 min). Swap actors' starting sides if the sheet
says so.

**Calm footage (end, 10 min):** same as the start; checks lighting drift.

**Restore the live pipeline** before leaving and confirm the dashboard is live.

## 8. Analysis, fixed before the session

Pre-register by committing this file and the run sheet before the day; the
analysis below does not change after seeing the data.

- **Trace:** replay the session through the unchanged edge loop at the true
  frame rate (from timestamps), with the configs in use on the day.
- **Event label:** first WARNING+ frame after each valid trial's slate; trials
  where WARNING never fires are reported separately (detection failures).
- **Primary metric:** anticipation AUROC at 2 s, ViT-g predictor, frame-level
  p95, conformal α = 0.05, calm windows only, block bootstrap by trial.
- **Primary comparison (H1):** O1 + O2 vs V1 anticipation AUROC.
- **Secondary (H2):** ViT-g vs pixel frame difference vs ViT-B ridge (fitted on
  the session's start-of-day calm footage), per condition.
- **Secondary (H3):** V2: does surprise rise when a parked car starts moving,
  before `v_veh_max` crosses the motion gate?
- **False alarms:** surprise runs during N trials and calm footage, per hour.
- **Exploratory only:** a wider AOI / no crop-zoom replay, so pedestrians are
  detected before entering the strip; region pooling.
